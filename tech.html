Technology, coercion & investigation

Technical knowledge. Survivor-informed purpose.

My interests connect education about digital coercion with software development, cybersecurity, digital investigations, and digital forensics.

The connection

Why technology is part of my advocacy.

I want to understand the technologies involved in exploitation deeply enough to contribute to better responses. That purpose guides my technical learning and professional development.

My interests include how digital systems can enable coercion and surveillance, how evidence can be understood in context, and how technical tools can better account for survivor safety.

01 / Digital coercion

Understanding technology-facilitated control.

I’m interested in educating others about digital coercion: the use of technology to monitor, pressure, threaten, or control someone. This includes understanding how control over accounts, communication, and private information can become part of abuse and exploitation.

My focus is on helping people recognize these patterns and understand their impact. I connect this educational interest with my technical development, with particular attention to privacy, autonomy, and survivors’ circumstances.

02 / Cybersecurity & privacy

Building knowledge with safety in mind.

I’m expanding my knowledge of cybersecurity, digital privacy, and ethical security testing. My interests include protecting accounts, devices, and sensitive information.

I’m especially interested in how security tools and practices can account for situations involving coercion, shared access, and technology-facilitated abuse.

03 / Digital investigations & forensics

Developing an investigative foundation.

My developing interests include open-source intelligence (OSINT), which involves evaluating information available through public sources. I’m also exploring digital forensics—the careful examination of data from devices and digital systems.

I want to build skills in identifying patterns, assessing information, and understanding digital evidence in context. Accuracy, privacy, and ethical practice are central to that direction.

My long-term goal is to connect these skills with survivor-informed work addressing technology-facilitated exploitation.

04 / Software development

Practical skills for building solutions.

I work with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript to build responsive websites and interactive web experiences. My experience includes page structure, styling, forms, and user interaction.

Development strengthens my understanding of how digital tools are built and gives me a practical foundation for future work in security and investigative technology.
