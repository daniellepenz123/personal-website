Survivor leadership & advocacy

Advocacy grounded in lived experience.

My work focuses on how exploitation is understood, how survivors are recognized and supported, and how their expertise shapes the response.

Survivor leadership

Lived experience belongs in leadership.

Meaningful participation. Professional respect. Influence over decisions.

01 / Awareness & understanding

Challenging incomplete narratives.

I challenge simplified narratives about human trafficking that overlook coercive control, technology-facilitated exploitation, and experiences that do not fit familiar stereotypes.

My advocacy calls for closer attention to patterns of exploitation, the context surrounding a person’s circumstances, and the barriers that can prevent their experience from being recognized.

I want awareness to support a more accurate understanding of trafficking and more informed responses to the people affected by it.

02 / Survivor leadership

Meaningful participation and influence.

I advocate for survivors to hold meaningful roles in leadership, research, education, technology, and program development.

Survivor-informed work draws on lived experience throughout planning and decision-making. Survivor-led work creates opportunities for survivors to set priorities and direct that work.

Both require respect for survivors’ expertise, autonomy, and professional boundaries.

03 / Survivor-informed responses

Examining gaps in existing systems.

My work brings attention to survivor identification, trauma-informed responses, and the challenges people encounter after exploitation.

I’m interested in how policies, services, and professional practices can better account for coercion, individual circumstances, and survivors’ own priorities.

My goal is to contribute to change that improves recognition, respects choice, and creates meaningful opportunities for survivors.

04 / Technology-facilitated exploitation

Recognizing digital coercion.

Education about digital coercion is a central part of my focus. I examine how technology can be used to monitor someone, control access to accounts, threaten to share private information, or restrict communication—and how these actions can form part of a broader pattern of coercive control.

I want to help people recognize these patterns and understand their connection to abuse and exploitation. This focus connects my advocacy with my interests in cybersecurity, digital investigations, and survivor safety.
